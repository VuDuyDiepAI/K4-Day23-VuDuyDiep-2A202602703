# World Models: A Survey of Architectures, Evaluation, and Open Problems

## TL;DR

- "World model" now names two different engineering programs: compact latent dynamics learned for control, rooted in Dyna and the recurrent state-space models of PlaNet and the Dreamer line [1][2][3][4][5][6], and value-equivalent predictive models such as MuZero and EfficientZero that never reconstruct observations at all [7][8].
- The 2024-2026 wave is dominated by generative, increasingly *interactive* simulators: Sora's diffusion transformer over spacetime patches [9], Genie 1 to Genie 3 (11B-parameter latent-action model to real-time 24 fps, 720p worlds) [10][11], single-TPU 20 fps DOOM in GameNGen [12], keyboard-controlled Oasis trained with Diffusion Forcing [13][14], persistent Gaussian-splat worlds from World Labs' Marble [15][16], and NVIDIA's Cosmos world-foundation-model stack [17][18].
- A parallel line predicts in *representation* space rather than pixels — V-JEPA 2 and RoboJEPA, the latter reporting that imagination error follows a second-order power law in compute across 12 robot embodiments [19][20].
- Evaluation is the field's bottleneck: benchmarks now exist for physics adherence [21][22][23], geometric coherence [24], multi-turn interactivity [25][26] and long-horizon agency [27], and they consistently find that visual fidelity decouples from world behaviour — the best model scored 57.76/100 on a 40-task physics benchmark [23], and no model performed strongly across all dimensions in a 289-case interactive benchmark [25].
- There is still no consensus definition; competing taxonomies split on whether a world model must be action-conditioned and interactive [28][29][30][31][32][33][34][35], and the newest safety results show that adversarial context images can collapse rollouts or break the alignment between what a world-action model imagines and what it executes [36][37].

## Background

The term is used in at least four communities, and the disagreement is substantive rather than cosmetic. A 2026 perspective article states plainly that world models "have become one of the most actively debated concepts in AI" with "no consensus on what a world model fundamentally is, what it should predict, or how it should be built", and proposes a definition via the partially observable Markov decision process (POMDP) — a world model as "a compression modeling of the state transition processes of the physical world, constructed under the constraints of finite computational resources", while insisting that photorealism is "neither necessary nor sufficient" for controllable physical understanding [34]. World Labs' own follow-up essay grounds the same POMDP agent loop in Sutton and Barto's reinforcement-learning textbook and traces the phrase to Kenneth Craik's 1943 proposal of internal "small-scale models" [33].

Historically the idea is older than deep learning. Dyna (Sutton, 1991; with Dyna-PI and Dyna-Q from ICML 1990) integrated learning, planning and reactive execution, where "planning is incremental and can use the probabilistic and ofttimes incorrect world models generated by learning processes" [1]; DreamerV2's full text explicitly credits that line of work for models that "predict the outcomes of potential actions to enable planning" [5]. A separate, cognitive-science root is predictive coding: hierarchical top-down predictions with bottom-up prediction-error residuals, generalized into free-energy/active-inference accounts of perception and action, reviewed specifically as an account of "the brain as a world model" [38]. Notably, that review also observes that most AI world-model applications at the time were "limited to simulation studies or adopt an offline pretrained world model", and that the corresponding theory "remains under development" [38] — an early statement of the simulation-to-reality gap that later interactive systems attack directly.

The canonical deep-learning instantiation is Ha and Schmidhuber's World Models: a convolutional VAE (V) compressing frames to latents of dimension 32 (Car Racing) or 64 (VizDoom), a mixture-density LSTM (M) predicting the next latent, and a linear controller (C) trained by CMA-ES, with most capacity in the world model rather than the policy: the paper's architecture section reports 4,348,547 and 422,368 parameters for V and M versus 867 for a single linear controller layer [39]. Those per-module counts come from the retrieved architecture text; an independent spot check of the abstract page at verification time did not surface them, so they are reported as retrieved rather than independently re-verified. The architecture was trained on 10,000 random-policy rollouts in under an hour on a single GPU, and the paper reports training the controller entirely inside the "hallucinated dream" of the MDN-RNN and transferring it back to the real VizDoom environment, with the model's temperature controlling the realism/exploitability trade-off [39][2]. Three design commitments from that work — unsupervised compression, a separately trained dynamics model, and policy learning inside the model — are still visible in every latent-dynamics agent below.

## Recurrent state-space dynamics and latent imagination

PlaNet replaced pixel-space planning with online planning in a learned latent space, introducing deterministic plus stochastic transition components and a multi-step variational objective called latent overshooting, and solving continuous-control contact and sparse-reward tasks from pixels at a level "close to and sometimes higher than strong model-free algorithms" [3]. Dreamer then replaced the derivative-free evolutionary policy search of the original World Models with analytic gradients of learned state values propagated back through imagined latent trajectories, reporting gains in data efficiency, computation time and final performance across 20 visual control tasks [4]. DreamerV2 switched to discrete latent representations and became, in its authors' words, the first agent to reach human-level performance on the 55-task Atari benchmark "by learning behaviors inside a separately trained world model", reaching 200M frames within the same compute budget and surpassing the final performance of strong single-GPU model-free agents; it also learned a humanoid world model sufficient for stand-up and walking from pixels alone [5]. DreamerV3 generalized this recipe with normalization, balancing and transformation techniques that let a single configuration outperform specialized methods across more than 150 tasks, and was the first algorithm to collect Minecraft diamonds from scratch without human data or curricula [6].

A structurally different answer to the same problem is value equivalence. MuZero keeps only an iterable latent state that predicts policy, value and reward, with "no direct constraint or requirement for the hidden state to capture all information necessary to reconstruct the original observation", combining that model with Monte-Carlo tree search to reach state-of-the-art on 57 Atari games and to match AlphaZero on Go, chess and shogi without being given the rules [7]. Earlier work had instead learned a reconstruction-oriented pixel dynamics model and used it inside a planner without search-based policy improvement [3]; MuZero's ablation showing that a model-free Q-learning objective (R2D2) learned "significantly slower … and converged to a much lower final score" on Ms. Pacman is direct evidence that search inside a learned model, not the model's fidelity alone, drives performance [7]. EfficientZero built on MuZero with self-supervised consistency and off-policy value correction, reaching 194.3% mean and 109.0% median human-normalized score on Atari 100k using about two hours of real-time experience and roughly 500× less data than DQN at 200M frames — the first super-human result on that budget [8].

This line is also where world models meet agentic evaluation. Crafter evaluates a spectrum of abilities in one procedurally generated open-world survival game, scoring semantically meaningful achievements so that "memorizing action sequences is not a viable strategy" [40]; follow-up work built generalization and meta-learning splits and found that "current agents struggle to generalize" to unseen numbers of objects within a 1M-step budget [41]. That is the recurrent failure mode of learned dynamics: competence inside the training distribution, brittleness outside it.

## Generative pixel-space world simulators

The 2024-2026 wave reframes world modelling as conditional video generation. Sora's technical report describes a diffusion transformer operating on "spacetime patches" of latent video codes, generating up to a minute of 1920x1080 or 1080x1920 video (images up to 2048x2048) and claiming emergent simulation properties — 3D consistency under camera motion, long-range coherence, object permanence, and zero-shot control of a Minecraft player — without explicit 3D inductive biases. It also acknowledges inaccurate physics in basic interactions and incoherent long samples, and explicitly excludes model and implementation details, reporting only qualitative evaluation [9].

Interactive generators then traded resolution and length for control. Genie 1 was an 11B-parameter foundation model combining a spatiotemporal video tokenizer, an autoregressive dynamics model and a scalable latent action model, trained on 200,000 hours of internet gaming video (filtered to 30,000 hours of 2D platformer gameplay, 942B tokens on 256 TPUv5p); it inferred latent actions so that users could act frame-by-frame without ground-truth action labels, but was limited to 16 frames of memory at roughly 1 FPS [10]. GameNGen ran a neural DOOM engine at 20 fps on a single TPU with next-frame PSNR of 29.4 and human raters near chance at distinguishing short clips after five minutes of autoregressive generation, using a two-phase recipe of training an RL agent and then a diffusion next-frame model on its recorded sessions [12]. Oasis took the opposite design route from Sora: a ViT spatial autoencoder plus a DiT latent-diffusion backbone that generates frames autoregressively conditioned on keyboard input, trained with Diffusion Forcing's independent per-token noise levels, running at 20 fps with a 500M-parameter checkpoint released and the framing "there is no physics engine; just a foundation model" [13][14]. Genie 3 closed much of the frame-rate gap, generating navigable environments at 24 fps and 720p with consistency over a few minutes and visual memory reaching back about a minute, and argues that this consistency is *emergent* from frame-by-frame autoregressive generation rather than backed by an explicit 3D representation such as a NeRF or Gaussian splat; it was used to host DeepMind's SIMA agent, which sends navigation actions that the model simulates without knowing the agent's goal, and it remains limited in action space and to minutes rather than hours of interaction [11].

A complementary strategy is to make the world *persistent* rather than regenerate it per frame. World Labs' Marble generates editable 3D worlds from text, images, video or coarse 3D layouts and exports them as Gaussian splats, meshes or videos [15]; coverage contrasts this explicitly with on-the-fly generators such as Genie, Decart and Odyssey, describes a "Chisel" editor that decouples structure from style, and reports commercial tiers and compatibility with Vision Pro and Quest 3 [16]. On the industrial side, Cosmos is presented as a platform of world foundation models rather than a single model: a video curation pipeline, tokenizers, pre-trained diffusion and autoregressive transformers over continuous and discrete latents respectively, and post-training recipes for camera control, manipulation and driving, released open-weight as Cosmos-Predict1 [17]. NVIDIA's own documentation organizes the ecosystem into cosmos-predict (world generation), cosmos-transfer (multi-ControlNet) and cosmos-reason (a reasoning VLM), with a flow-based Predict2.5 that unifies text-, image- and video-to-world generation and uses Cosmos-Reason1 as its text encoder, and a Transfer2.5 accepting RGB, depth and segmentation control maps [18].

## Prediction in latent space, and scaling evidence

A third family avoids pixels as the prediction target. V-JEPA 2 is an action-free joint-embedding predictive architecture pre-trained on over a million hours of internet video, reporting 77.3 top-1 on Something-Something v2, 39.7 recall-at-5 on Epic-Kitchens-100 action anticipation, and 84.0 on PerceptionTest and 76.9 on TempCompass at 8B scale after alignment with an LLM; its action-conditioned variant V-JEPA 2-AC is post-trained with under 62 hours of unlabelled Droid robot video to enable planning [19]. RoboJEPA extends the JEPA recipe to 12 robotic embodiments and reports that imagination error — the error of its latent rollouts — follows a second-order power law in compute, that downstream planning performance tracks compute predictably, and that the latent world model can be deployed zero-shot to plan toward a single goal image on real hardware, at 8B parameters the largest JEPA predictor to date [20]. Diffusion Forcing supplies the training-time ingredient that makes long autoregressive rollouts feasible in either space, denoising tokens with independent noise levels so that causal next-token prediction inherits full-sequence diffusion's guidance; its authors report stable rollout of roughly 1000 frames past the training horizon where teacher-forcing and full-sequence baselines diverge, plus Monte-Carlo guidance for high-reward sampling, while noting the causal implementation is RNN-based and internet-scale scaling was not studied [14].

Scaling in latent space is not automatically safe, however. A 2026 result shows that next-step prediction with anti-collapse regularization "does not guarantee that controllable unstable modes are preserved": the training loss can be low while the representation discards exactly the unstable modes a controller must observe [42]. A more fundamental identifiability result studies when governing equations can be uniquely identified from finite observations and finds that models "may fit the available data perfectly, but lack genuine predictive power"; discoverability requires chaos (systems chaotic on their whole domain are discoverable from a single trajectory), while "analytic discoverability is impossible in the presence of first integrals, common in real-world systems" — and the authors warn that chaotic benchmarks may have inadvertently obscured this limitation [43]. Together these results reframe "scaling laws for world models" as a statement about *rollout error on the evaluated distribution*, not about recovering the true dynamics.

## Domain world models: driving, robotics and embodied agents

Application-specific world models impose structured conditioning that general video models lack. Vista, a generalizable driving world model, uses a latent replacement mechanism to inject historical frames as priors for coherent long-horizon rollouts, supports control from high-level command and goal points down to trajectory, angle and speed, and reports beating the most advanced general-purpose video generator in over 70% of comparisons and the best driving world model by 55% in FID and 27% in FVD, while also serving as a generalizable reward model for real-world action evaluation without ground-truth actions [44]. GAIA-2 generates multi-camera, spatiotemporally consistent driving video conditioned on ego dynamics, agent configurations, environmental factors and road semantics, across UK, US and German environments, and integrates external latent embeddings from a proprietary driving model [45]. Its framing of why general video models are insufficient — driving demands fine-grained control over ego actions, other agents and contextual attributes, plus consistent multi-camera streams — generalizes to other embodied domains [45].

In robotics, RoboDreamer attacks compositionality directly: instead of conditioning one video model on a full language instruction, it parses instructions into primitives and factorizes generation across models conditioned on them, so unseen combinations can be composed, and additional multimodal goals such as a goal image can be combined with language; the authors report synthesizing video plans for unseen goals in RT-X and outperforming monolithic baselines [46]. DreamTrue targets action faithfulness across embodiments, rendering action trajectories into image-space conditions and adding offline geometric calibration to align them with target videos, plus counterfactual post-training to broaden interaction coverage, motivated by the observations that imprecise calibration impairs action following and that limited coverage of unsuccessful interactions biases predictions toward successes [47]. A 2026 embodied-video benchmark reaches the same conclusion from the evaluation side, reporting "significant gaps in physical realism" for video generation models used as embodied experience [48].

Two surveys of this subfield converge on the same emphasis. The robotics-focused survey frames world models as "predictive representations of how environments evolve under actions" supporting policy learning, planning, simulation, evaluation and data generation, and states that the literature "remains fragmented across architectures, functional roles, and embodied application domains" [49]; the driving survey organizes approaches by the modality of the predicted scene and reports the largest curated resource list in the field [50]. The newest wrinkle is that world modelling is escaping pixels entirely: Trace2Env makes a language-model *agent* serve as the environment for a task agent, reconstructing historical interaction traces into a reusable "worldbook" so that stateful simulation is possible when the original system is unavailable, evaluated across nine environments and appearing among the top-ranked world-model items on the Hugging Face daily papers feed at retrieval (the feed's vote count could not be re-confirmed on the paper page during verification, so momentum is asserted only qualitatively) [51].

## How world models are evaluated

Evaluation has bifurcated into perceptual-quality suites and behaviour-facing suites. VBench is the reference point for the former: it decomposes video-generation quality into 16 disentangled dimensions with concrete metrics (DINO-based subject consistency, CLIP-based background consistency, RAFT-based dynamic degree, LAION aesthetic and MUSIQ imaging quality, UMT human-action and ViCLIP overall consistency), with roughly 100 prompts per dimension and human preference annotations [52]. Critically, VBench measures perceptual, temporal and alignment quality — not physics or causality — and later benchmarks target exactly that gap [52].

Physics-facing benchmarks report low ceilings. Physics-IQ requires models to predict how a video continues over the next five seconds across fluid dynamics, optics, solid mechanics, magnetism and thermodynamics, and concludes that "physical understanding is severely limited, and unrelated to visual realism" across the systems tested [21]. A 2026 audit of that benchmark refines 57.6% of its samples and improves over 34.8% of its prompts, producing "moderate but meaningful ranking changes" (Kendall's tau = 0.46) across six image-to-video models, and criticizes distributional metrics such as Frechet Video Distance for comparing unmatched sets rather than controlled physical experiments [53]. A separate 2026 benchmark with 40 controlled tasks in nine physical categories and 1,280 generated videos finds persistent physical inconsistencies with a best overall score of 57.76 out of 100 [23]. WorldModelBench formalizes the "does this deserve to be called a world model" question with 350 image-text condition pairs over 7 domains and 56 subdomains, scoring instruction following and physics adherence as five binary violation types, and documents concrete mass/Newton violations; it used 67K crowd-sourced human labels to measure 14 frontier models and reports that a fine-tuned 2B judge outperformed GPT-4o at predicting world-modelling violations (the abstract and paper versions of that comparison differ, so the margin should be read as reported rather than settled) [22]. A controlled study of physical-law generalization in a 2D collision testbed reports perfect generalization within the training distribution and measurable scaling for combinatorial generalization, but failure in out-of-distribution scenarios: models "fail to abstract general physical rules and instead exhibit 'case-based' generalization behaviour", retrieving the closest training example and prioritizing colour over size, velocity and shape, which leads the authors to conclude that "scaling alone is insufficient for video generation models to uncover fundamental physical laws" [54]. WorldScore unifies 3D, 4D and video world generation over 3,000 examples scored on controllability, quality and dynamics, and demonstrates the practical payoff of multi-scene evaluation: it separates two models that single-scene video benchmarks rate similarly, by detecting that one fails to generate a new scene or to follow instructed camera motion [55].

The newest benchmarks evaluate world behaviour rather than frames. WBench scores 20 state-of-the-art models over 289 cases and 1,058 interaction turns across five dimensions (video quality, setting adherence, interaction adherence, consistency, physics compliance) with 22 automatic sub-metrics validated against human judgments, and its headline result is that "no single model performs strongly across all dimensions" [25]; the accompanying Hub dataset card hosts the public leaderboard with a snapshot updated on 2026/10/04 and reports a separate project-page run over 43 models, again concluding that no model dominates [26]. WorldExam separates "apparent appearance" from "inherent reactivity" and finds that high visual fidelity coexists with missing reactive capability [56]. PDI-Bench audits geometric coherence by lifting object-centric segmentations and point tracks (SAM 2, MegaSaM, CoTracker3) into 3D and computing projective-geometry residuals over scale-depth alignment, 3D motion consistency and structural rigidity, exposing "geometry-specific failure modes that are not captured by common perceptual metrics" [24]. PlayWorld reframes evaluation as multi-modal agent players pursuing long-horizon objectives in 171 scenarios across four axes (geometry consistency, interaction fidelity, out-of-sight evolution, insight evolution), because a fixed action sequence cannot reach the same objective across models, and finds that nine state-of-the-art models "remain unreliable on long-horizon interactive objectives, particularly in maintaining spatial consistency and persistent state evolution" [27].

Two narrower diagnostics identify mechanisms rather than scores. WROP probes object permanence with 150 cognitive-science-inspired tasks, a 1.5M-sample training corpus and a 300-question exam over 14 video models, arguing that object permanence must be explicitly trained rather than assumed [57]. A 2026 diagnosis argues that imagined rollouts are "kinematic rather than dynamic": measured kinematic-consistency error stays flat while policy rewards collapse across friction boundaries, so visual consistency in imagination can conceal dynamics failure [58]. On the model-based RL side, where world models are evaluated by the control performance they enable, Crafter and its generalization splits remain the closest thing to a standard agentic yardstick [40][41].

## Definitions, taxonomies and the surrounding ecosystem

Independent surveys have converged on definitional fragmentation as the field's core problem. The earliest umbrella survey framed the debate as "is Sora a world simulator?" while surveying video generation, autonomous driving and autonomous agents together [59]. "Understanding World or Predicting Future?" organizes the field by a two-function split — world models that understand dynamics versus those that predict futures — across generative games, driving, robotics and social simulation [28]. The embodied-AI survey formalizes world models as internal simulators supporting forward and counterfactual rollouts for perception, prediction and decision making, with a multi-axis taxonomy whose first axis is functionality (decision-coupled versus general-purpose) [29]. The 3D/4D survey states the problem bluntly: "the absence of a standardized definition and taxonomy for 'world models' has led to fragmented and sometimes inconsistent claims in the literature" [31], while a recent umbrella survey motivates itself with the observation that "the field lacks a unified framework integrating its diverse architectural choices, training methods, reasoning mechanisms, and application settings" [30]. A 2026 survey of "World Action Models" documents the newest branch — systems that couple future-state prediction to action generation, balancing representational richness against compute [60] — and the robot-learning survey frames the same space by how world models are coupled to policies [49].

Position work takes sides rather than mapping. World Labs' essays define world models by three essential capabilities — generative, multimodal and interactive, with the interactive capability specified as outputting next states conditioned on input actions and consistent with previous state, goal, semantics, physical laws and dynamics [32] — and then propose a functional taxonomy of renderer (outputs observations, judged on fidelity: text-to-video models, Genie 3, RTFM), simulator (outputs structural state with geometry, physics and dynamics that agents, controllers and planners can compute on) and planner (outputs actions given observation and goal, e.g. vision-language-action models and world-action models), arguing all three are projections of one underlying understanding and are converging [33]. The same essay makes a data argument that the benchmarks corroborate: renderers are "awash in internet video while simulators and planners face acute shortages of 3D assets and robot demonstrations" [33]. The roadmap paper reformulates that taxonomy into computational roles and places systems on a many-to-many map (Sora and Seedance as observation-level renderers; Genie 3 extended toward interactive rollouts; the JEPA family as latent-space simulators; Dreamer as latent generative state-space; Marble bridging rendering and simulation via Gaussian splats plus collider meshes), while arguing that action-free prediction learns observational dynamics whereas action-conditioned prediction approximates interventional dynamics [34]. Against all of this sits a deliberately narrower, interpretability-flavoured definition that gives operational criteria for when a network represents state at all and explicitly does *not* require action simulation: "it's enough to represent the current state of the world in some nontrivial way" [35]. A NeurIPS 2025 position paper adds a further axis, arguing that physical and social dynamics are advanced "predominantly in separate silos" and splitting interactive from non-interactive dynamics, with social simulation described as underdeveloped relative to physics-shaped world models [61].

Ecosystem signals are consistent with the research emphasis. The world-model items returned by the Hugging Face daily papers feed at retrieval were concentrated in agentic language world modelling [51], with action-conditioned robot video world models directly alongside them [47][20], and the Hub now hosts both open-weights interactive generators — Waypoint-1.5 claims up to 720p at 60 fps on RTX 3090-5090 class hardware with a 360p tier, a World Engine inference library and weights released [62] — and their limitations sections, with Waypoint-1-Small's own card documenting that "generations" may ignore the text prompt or controls and fail on text, HUD elements, faces and realistic motion, unlike mere continuations [63]. Benchmarks are distributed the same way, with public leaderboards and dataset cards rather than only papers [26]. Curated resource lists also indicate which domains dominate: the driving survey's companion paper list was the largest repository encountered in this retrieval, ahead of robotics/embodied AI and video/games, with social simulation claimed but, per the position paper, underdeveloped [50][29][49][61].

## Trends and open problems

**Fidelity is decoupling from world behaviour.** The strongest single empirical regularity in the retrieved literature is that perceptual quality and physical/dynamic correctness rise together only weakly. Physics-IQ found physical understanding "unrelated to visual realism" [21]; the physics-consistency benchmark tops out at 57.76/100 [23]; WorldExam finds high visual fidelity with missing reactivity [56]; PlayWorld finds unreliable spatial consistency and persistent state [27]; WROP finds object permanence insufficiencies that scale alone does not remove [57]; and the kinematic-versus-dynamic diagnosis explains one mechanism, namely that imagination can remain visually consistent while failing to respect the dynamics that matter [58]. A controlled physical-law study adds a generalization-specific version of the same finding, attributing out-of-distribution failures to case-based retrieval of the nearest training example rather than to abstract rule learning [54]. Future benchmarks should therefore report behaviour-facing metrics, and the audit of Physics-IQ shows why the measurement layer itself needs auditing: refining 57.6% of samples shifted model rankings with Kendall's tau of 0.46 [53].

**Interactive control is becoming the definitional dividing line.** Genie 1's latent action model, Oasis's keyboard conditioning, GameNGen's action-conditioned diffusion and Genie 3's real-time loop all treat action-conditioning as the point of the exercise [10][13][12][11], while the value-equivalence branch treats reconstruction as unnecessary and search as the mechanism [7][8]. The roadmap paper's distinction between observational and interventional dynamics [34] and the NeurIPS position paper's interactive/non-interactive split [61] give this a formal shape, but the field lacks a shared operational criterion: one prominent definition deliberately excludes action simulation altogether [35], which means cross-paper claims about "world models" are frequently not about the same object.

**Scaling laws are being written for rollouts, not for truth.** The most promising scaling evidence comes from latent prediction: RoboJEPA reports a second-order power law in compute for imagination error across 12 embodiments and uses it as a proxy for real-robot evaluation [20], and Diffusion Forcing shows that per-token noise schedules extend stable rollout roughly an order of magnitude past the training horizon [14]. But two theoretical results bound what scaling can deliver: low next-step loss does not guarantee that controllable unstable modes survive in the representation [42], and identification of the true system from finite observations is fundamentally non-unique, impossible in the presence of first integrals and dependent on chaos for discoverability [43]. Systems also need not be passive: research on observation limits proves that under text-only observation no monitoring system can reliably distinguish honest outputs from plausible fabrications, while richer per-token signals restore some detectability — an argument that evaluation and monitoring must consume more than the rendered output [64].

**Data and interface asymmetries remain.** The taxonomy essay notes renderers are data-rich while simulators and planners are starved of 3D assets and robot demonstrations [33], which matches the engineering choices visible in the recent literature: V-JEPA 2-AC needed under 62 hours of robot video on top of a million hours of internet video [19], and DreamTrue's design starts from the fact that existing robot datasets have imprecise calibration and cover mostly successful interactions [47]. Simulation platforms respond by making the data stack explicit — curation pipelines, tokenizers and post-training recipes for camera, manipulation and driving control [17][18] — and by generating structured multi-camera driving video as data [45][44]. Whether synthetic world-model data closes the gap or reintroduces physical biases is unresolved by the retrieved evidence.

**Safety is now a world-model problem, not just a policy problem.** BadWorld shows label-free adversarial perturbations in a single context image can trigger incomplete denoising, structural collapse and control inconsistency in autoregressive visual world models, without access to ground-truth futures — making the context image an attack surface for every planning pipeline built on top of it [36]. BadWAM attacks the safety assumption that a world-action model can check its actions against its own imagined futures: world-action drift attacks reduce closed-loop success from 96.5% to 43.1%, and an imagination-preserving variant keeps the predicted future close to the clean one while the executed action shifts — precisely the failure that internal consistency checking cannot catch [37]. Coupled with the observation-limit result [64], this suggests world-model safety will require instrumentation of internal state and dynamics, not only output inspection.

**Unification is the stated ambition, with fragmentation as the current state.** Multiple independent surveys name fragmentation as the primary obstacle [30][31][49], and the position literature proposes that renderers, simulators and planners are projections of one understanding that will converge [33][34], that physical and social dynamics should be unified under shared principles [61], and that world-action models are the emerging interface between prediction and control [60]. The evidence for convergence so far is architectural rather than empirical: latent action models, flow-matching and per-token diffusion schedules, and latent-space prediction are being combined in different orders by different groups, and interactive world models are increasingly distributed as artifacts — weights, engines, Spaces and public leaderboards — rather than as papers alone [51][20][47][62][63][26][18]. The field's most useful next step would be a shared, behavior-facing evaluation contract that separates appearance, reactivity, physical adherence and long-horizon state persistence, so that claims made under incompatible definitions can at least be measured on the same axes [25][26][27][23][53].
