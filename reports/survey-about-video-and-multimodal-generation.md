# Video and Multimodal Generation: Architectures, Unified Models, and Evaluation

## TL;DR

- Video generation converged on one dominant recipe — a spatio-temporal (3D/wavelet) VAE plus a diffusion transformer over latent spacetime patches — a lineage that runs from pixel-space 3D U-Nets [1][2] through latent video diffusion [3][4][5] and the diffusion transformer [6][7] to Sora's spacetime patches [8] and today's open DiT systems [9][10][11].
- Open-weight releases have largely caught up in capability: HunyuanVideo trained a >13B open model that beat Runway Gen-3 and Luma 1.6 in human evaluation [10], CogVideoX generates 10-second clips [9], and Wan ships 14B and 1.3B variants, the latter needing only 8.19 GB of VRAM, while still acknowledging "suboptimal performance", "limited capabilities" and "insufficient efficiency" versus commercial systems [11].
- The research frontier moved to latency and duration: LTX-Video generates 5 s at 768x512 in 2 s on one H100 [12], Self-Forcing reaches sub-second streaming latency by training with autoregressive rollout [13], and Rolling Forcing streams multi-minute video in real time on a single GPU [14].
- Unified any-to-any multimodal generation has split into three design families — fully discrete early fusion [15][16], hybrid next-token-prediction plus diffusion/flow [17][18], and decoupled visual encoders [19][20] — and its newest axis is native joint audio-video generation [21][22][23].
- Evaluation has moved beyond FVD/IS to disentangled, physics-aware benchmarks [24][25][26][27], but a benchmark built on real-world footage concludes that visual realism does not imply physical understanding [28], a controlled simulation study finds that scaling alone fails out-of-distribution physical generalisation [29], and judge-based and human protocols remain fragile and non-standardised [30][31].

## Background

"Video generation" here means generative models that synthesise temporally coherent image sequences, usually conditioned on text or an image, while "multimodal generation" means systems that both consume and produce more than one modality (text, image, video, audio) either in one transformer or through a cascade of decoders. The two literatures have been converging: autoregressive video models inherit their objectives from language modelling [32][16], and unified multimodal models add video and audio streams to what began as image-plus-text systems [33][21].

Three constraints shape the field. First, temporal modelling: video quality is not per-frame image quality, and the dimensions that matter (motion smoothness, flicker, subject consistency, camera motion) must be measured separately [24][34]. Second, cost: open releases compete explicitly on resource footprint — Wan's 1.3B model is offered at 8.19 GB of VRAM for consumer-grade GPUs [11] — and a substantial sub-literature exists purely to accelerate inference through sparse attention [35] and few-step distillation [36]. Third, data: Stable Video Diffusion frames the field's status as image LDMs converted to video by "inserting temporal layers and finetuning them on small, high-quality video datasets", with no agreed curation strategy [5], and the standard survey of video diffusion models lists generating longer videos and computational cost as the field's headline challenges [37].

## Foundations: from pixel-space video diffusion to diffusion transformers

The earliest text-conditioned video generators were direct extensions of image diffusion. Video Diffusion Models (VDM) factorised a 3D U-Net over space and time — 2D 3x3 convolutions become 1x3x3, and a temporal attention block follows each spatial attention block — and showed that joint image-video training (masking temporal attention so the model also runs on independent images) reduces gradient variance and improves sample quality; it trained on 10 million captioned videos [1]. Imagen Video pushed resolution by cascading a base video model with interleaved spatial and temporal super-resolution stages and applying progressive distillation with classifier-free guidance [2]. Make-A-Video established the complementary recipe of inflating a pretrained text-to-image model and learning motion from unpaired video, decomposing full temporal U-Net and attention tensors into spatial and temporal approximations and adding temporal interpolation and super-resolution [38].

Efficiency drove the next step: MagicVideo generated 256x256 clips in a low-dimensional latent space with a 3D U-Net, reporting roughly 64x fewer FLOPs than VDM and explicitly contrasting itself with training video models in RGB space [3]. Align your Latents formalised this as "pretrain an image LDM, then add a temporal dimension and finetune on encoded videos", also temporally aligning the diffusion upsamplers [4]; ModelScopeT2V packaged a comparable recipe (VQGAN, text encoder, denoising U-Net, ~1.7B parameters) and could adapt to varying frame counts [39]; Stable Video Diffusion scaled it to high-resolution text-to-video and image-to-video with a three-stage training and data-curation programme [5].

In parallel, a token-based autoregressive line existed from the start: CogVideo inherited a pretrained text-to-image transformer (CogView2) and added frame interpolation, at 9B parameters and 5.4M text-video pairs [40]; Phenaki used a causal video tokeniser plus a masked transformer to generate variable-length narratives from prompt sequences [41]; VideoPoet showed that a decoder-only transformer with a mixture of multimodal generative objectives can synthesise video with matching audio from text, images, video or audio [32].

The transformer then replaced the U-Net. DiT showed that replacing the U-Net with a transformer over latent patches makes diffusion scale predictably — higher-Gflops DiTs consistently have lower FID, reaching FID 2.27 on ImageNet 256x256 [6]. Latte carried this into video, extracting spatio-temporal tokens and modelling them with transformer blocks in latent space, with four variants that decompose the spatial and temporal axes to cope with token counts [7]. Lumiere took the opposite route to temporal consistency from Imagen Video's cascade: a Space-Time U-Net that generates the entire duration in a single pass, arguing that keyframe-plus-temporal-super-resolution inherently struggles with global coherence [42]. Sora's technical report articulates the resulting consensus: a diffusion transformer operating on spacetime patches of video and image latent codes, trained jointly on videos and images of variable duration, resolution and aspect ratio, generating up to a minute of video at native 1920x1080 with emergent 3D consistency and long-range coherence — while explicitly noting failures of physical interaction and inconsistent state changes, and withholding model and implementation details [8]. Transfusion supplied the design bridge to multimodal models, combining next-token prediction for discrete text with diffusion on continuous image patches inside one transformer and reporting that this scales better than quantising images [17]. VAR, in the image domain, is the strongest counter-evidence for the pure-diffusion consensus: next-scale prediction improved ImageNet 256x256 FID from 18.65 to 1.73 and IS from 80.4 to 350.2 with 20x faster inference than the autoregressive baseline, and the authors position it as the first GPT-style model to surpass diffusion transformers, with 3D next-scale prediction proposed for video [43].

## Open-weight video generators and the scaling race (2024-2025)

CogVideoX marks the point where the Sora recipe became reproducible: a diffusion transformer with a 3D VAE that compresses video along both spatial and temporal axes, generating 10-second continuous clips at 16 fps and 768x1360 [9]. HunyuanVideo then scaled openly, training a >13B-parameter model — described as the largest among open-source models at the time — and beating Runway Gen-3, Luma 1.6 and three top Chinese systems in professional human evaluation [10]. Open-Sora Plan contributed an open pipeline with a Wavelet-Flow VAE, a joint image-video skiparse denoiser and condition controllers, plus a multi-dimensional data-curation pipeline [44]. Wan shipped a full open suite (14B and 1.3B) built on a spatio-temporal VAE with scalable pre-training and automated evaluation metrics, claiming the 14B model outperforms existing open-source and state-of-the-art commercial solutions, and that the 1.3B model runs in 8.19 GB of VRAM and is the first to render visual text in both Chinese and English [11]. HunyuanVideo 1.5 pushed the same family toward deployment, with 8.3B parameters, selective and sliding tile attention, glyph-aware text encoding and an efficient super-resolution network for consumer GPUs, claiming open-source state of the art against closed systems and against Wan 2.2's mixture-of-experts design (27B total, 14B activated) [45].

Read together, these reports describe a single maturing template — spatio-temporal VAE + DiT + progressive (low-resolution image pre-training first) training + curated data + an automated evaluation harness [9][10][44][11][45] — and a shared framing of the remaining gap as data, task coverage and compute rather than architecture [10][11].

## Efficiency, streaming and autoregressive video diffusion

Efficiency work attacks cost at three levels. At the representation level, LTX-Video uses a Video-VAE with a 1:192 compression ratio (32x32x8 pixels per token), relocates patchification into the VAE input to allow full spatiotemporal self-attention in latent space, and lets the decoder perform the final denoising step in pixel space; it generates 5 seconds of 24 fps video at 768x512 in 2 seconds on an H100 [12]. At the attention level, Sparse VideoGen2 is a training-free sparse-attention framework that diagnoses prior sparsity as mis-identifying critical tokens by position rather than semantics, then clusters and permutes tokens with k-means and a top-p budget, reporting up to 2.30x speedup at PSNR 30 on HunyuanVideo and 1.89x at PSNR 26 on Wan 2.1 [35]. At the sampling level, AccVideo distils a few-step student from denoising trajectories turned into a synthetic dataset, reporting 8.5x faster generation while producing 5-second 720x1280 video at 24 fps [36].

A second cluster rethinks the causal structure of video generation itself. Self-Forcing targets the train-test gap of autoregressive video diffusion: instead of denoising future frames from ground-truth context, it conditions each frame on previously self-generated outputs via autoregressive rollout with KV caching during training, enabling a holistic video-level loss, and uses a few-step model with stochastic gradient truncation plus a rolling KV cache; the result is real-time streaming with sub-second latency on a single GPU that matches or surpasses non-causal diffusion teachers [13]. Rolling Forcing extends streaming to multi-minute horizons with three mechanisms — joint denoising of frames at progressively increasing noise levels to relax strict causality, an attention sink that keeps initial-frame key-value states as global context, and few-step distillation over extended denoising windows on non-overlapping windows to mitigate exposure bias [14]. FramePack attacks the same drift problem from the data layout side, packing frame context by estimated frame importance so more frames fit in a fixed context length, enabling inference with thousands of frames, and proposing drift-prevention tricks (early-established endpoints, adjusted sampling order, discrete history representation) framed as a "forgetting vs drifting" trade-off [46]. The interactive world-model branch applies the same ingredients to control: Matrix-Game 2.0 builds ~1,200 hours of Unreal Engine and GTA5 data with interaction annotations, injects frame-level keyboard and mouse actions, and distils a causal architecture for 25 FPS minute-level streaming [47].

## Unified any-to-any multimodal generation

The pre-2024 baseline for any-to-any generation was a cascade: NExT-GPT wires an LLM to multimodal adaptors and diffusion decoders so that both inputs and outputs can take any modality [48]. Unified-IO 2 instead tokenised everything — image, text, audio, action — into a shared space processed by a single encoder-decoder transformer, claiming to be the first autoregressive model that both understands and generates all four [33]. Today's models divide into three families.

Fully discrete early fusion: Chameleon represents images, text and code as one token stream, trained from scratch on roughly 10T interleaved tokens, where a 512x512 image becomes 1024 tokens from an 8192-entry codebook; it reports state-of-the-art captioning and competitive text performance, but its authors note that reconstruction of text-heavy images is bounded by the discrete tokeniser [15]. Emu3 pushes this to the limit — a single transformer trained solely with next-token prediction over tokenised image, text and video, claimed to outperform SDXL and LLaVA-1.6 on generation and perception tasks respectively [16] — and Show-o mixes autoregressive modelling with discrete diffusion in one transformer to cover VQA, text-to-image, inpainting/extrapolation and mixed-modality generation [49]. Emu3.5 continues this line as a "world learner" that predicts next states in vision and language with reinforcement learning post-training and discrete diffusion adaptation for faster inference [50].

Hybrid objectives: Transfusion keeps images continuous and pairs next-token prediction with diffusion in a single transformer [17], while BAGEL splits capacity rather than modality, using a mixture-of-transformer-experts in which a generation expert and an understanding expert share self-attention, with separate visual encoders for understanding and generation, and releasing code and checkpoints [18].

Decoupled visual encoding: Janus separates the visual encoder for understanding from the one for generation, arguing that the two tasks need different granularity [19]; Janus-Pro scales that design with a SigLIP-based understanding encoder and a discrete VQ tokeniser for generation plus large-scale real and synthetic data for both halves, reporting improved generation-alignment and understanding scores over the earlier Janus while noting a fixed low-resolution understanding input and limited fine detail in generated images as limitations [20]. NExT-OMNI argues that both hybrids and decoupled designs are "redundant and non-integrated" and replaces them with discrete flow matching as a single objective [51].

These models are scored on two different halves. Generation quality is measured by object-focused compositional alignment: GenEval was introduced precisely because FID and CLIPScore are unsuited to fine-grained, instance-level analysis [52], and DPG-Bench supplies lengthy dense prompts averaging about 83.9 tokens, far above T2I-CompBench's ~12.65 [53]. Understanding is measured by suites such as MMMU's 11.5K college-level questions across six disciplines [54]. A structural caveat: most of the 2024-2025 "unified" models above are image-plus-text systems, with video appearing mainly as training data or as one more token stream [15][16][18][50].

## Native audio-video and omnimodal systems

The most recent expansion is joint audio-video generation, which makes synchronisation a first-class constraint rather than a post-hoc step. Ovi models both modalities in one generative process with twin DiT backbones fused blockwise — timing via scaled-RoPE embeddings and semantics via bidirectional cross-attention — conditioned by a single frozen T5 encoder, with the audio tower initialised from a video-model architecture and trained on hundreds of thousands of hours of audio, and positions itself as an open alternative to closed systems such as Veo3 [21]. JavisDiT adds a hierarchical spatio-temporal synchronised-prior estimator to guide audio-visual alignment and introduces JavisBench, 10,140 captioned sounding videos with a synchrony metric [22]. 3MDiT models video, audio and text as jointly evolving streams with dynamic text conditioning in a unified tri-modal diffusion transformer [23].

Alongside these, the "omni" line emphasises understanding plus one generated modality: Baichuan-Omni-1.5 pairs omni-modal understanding with end-to-end audio generation using an audio tokeniser that captures semantic and acoustic information, trained on a ~500B-sample pipeline [55], while Qwen2.5-Omni streams text, images, audio and video as input and emits text plus speech via a dual-track architecture, illustrating the boundary between omni understanding with speech synthesis and true visual generation [56]. That boundary matters for evaluation: these systems are largely scored on understanding-side multimodal benchmarks such as MMMU [54] and on text-to-video benchmarks such as VBench [24], neither of which measures the fidelity of the audio a joint model synthesises.

## Evaluation of generative video and multimodal systems

Evaluation began as a single number and became a taxonomy. EvalCrafter was an early argument that FVD and IS cannot capture models with multi-aspect abilities, proposing visual, content, motion and textual quality families [25], and FETV added category-conditional and temporally aware prompt taxonomies, arguing that prior prompt categorisations either focus on one aspect or ignore temporal information [57]. VBench then decomposed quality into 16 named dimensions with per-dimension human-preference annotations, on the premise that existing metrics do not fully align with human perception [24]; its extension adds an image-to-video suite, a trustworthiness axis, and full release of prompts, videos and annotations [24]. AIGVE surveys the resulting landscape and groups benchmarks into five families by focus (general large-scale, hierarchical/multi-dimensional, temporal- and motion-centric, and real-world-oriented), concluding that no single benchmark captures all aspects and that temporal modelling remains a core challenge [34].

The sharpest recent shift is from surface realism to intrinsic faithfulness. VBench-2.0 argues that per-frame aesthetics, temporal consistency and basic prompt adherence constitute only "superficial faithfulness", and evaluates five categories — Human Fidelity, Controllability, Creativity, Physics and Commonsense — across 18 sub-dimensions, with physics sub-dimensions for mechanics, thermotics, material and multi-view consistency, using generalist VLM/LLM judges plus specialist detectors and safeguards such as pre-filtering and redundant questioning to contain judge hallucination [26]. PhyGenBench tests 160 prompts spanning 27 physical laws in four domains with a hierarchical evaluation framework, and reports both that current models struggle with physical commonsense and that scaling up models or prompt engineering is insufficient [27]. Physics-IQ takes the ground-truth route: instead of asking a judge whether a clip looks plausible, it films real-world scenarios spanning solid mechanics, fluid dynamics, optics, thermodynamics and magnetism and scores a continuation-prediction task against what actually happened, with the authors' headline conclusion that visual realism does not imply physical understanding [28]. The ICML physical-law testbed isolates scaling behaviour more cleanly: in a deterministic 2D mechanics simulation, diffusion-based video generators generalise perfectly in distribution, scale measurably for combinatorial generalisation, but fail out of distribution, appearing to imitate the closest training example and prioritising cues in the order colour > size > velocity > shape [29]. WorldScore reformulates the target as next-scene generation, scoring controllability, quality and dynamics, i.e. treating the model as an interactive world simulator rather than a clip generator [58].

Because no hand-crafted metric family survived contact with these failures, learned judges and reward models became the operational standard. Video-Bench uses chain-of-query probing and few-shot scoring to align MLLM judges with human ratings, and documents two failure modes of judge models: cross-modal hallucination when checking video-condition alignment, and ambiguity in converting textual critiques into concrete scores — distinguishing "very blurry" from "somewhat blurry", for example [30]. VideoScore2 predicts multi-dimensional scores with explicit rationales, which also makes it usable as an RL reward signal [59], and VisionReward learns a decomposed multi-dimensional human-preference model for images and video while criticising earlier reward models for "black-box scoring without interpretability and potentially resultant unexpected biases" [60]. Human evaluation itself has become a research object: T2VHE documents inconsistent metrics, methods and annotator sources across papers, laboratory-recruited annotators without quality checks, annotation counts spanning a few dozen to tens of thousands, and O(N^2) growth in comparison-based protocols, proposes six metrics including an ethical-robustness axis absent from earlier protocols, and reports a roughly 50% annotation cost reduction via dynamic selection while finding a substantial open-versus-closed quality disparity [31]. Provenance is the newest evaluation axis: Video Seal jointly trains an open embedder and extractor with codec-aware multistage training and temporal propagation, targeting efficiency, imperceptibility and robustness to compression and temporal attacks [61].

## Trends and open problems

**Consolidation, then competition on efficiency.** The DiT-plus-spatio-temporal-VAE recipe is now shared across open and closed systems [8][9][10][11][45], so differentiation has moved to data curation, post-training, and inference cost [11][36][35]. The clearest open problem is the compute envelope: the strongest open models remain expensive to run [11], which is exactly what the acceleration literature — sparse attention [35], few-step distillation [36] and aggressively compressed latent representations [12] — exists to relieve.

**A second paradigm shift toward causal and streaming generation.** Treating video generation as next-frame prediction with autoregressive rollouts [13][14][46] turns the field's central technical problem into exposure bias and drift — the mismatch between teacher-forced training and self-generated histories [13][46]. The published results suggest it is now competitive: sub-second latency single-GPU streaming that matches non-causal teachers [13] and multi-minute real-time streams [14], with interactivity as the payoff [47]. How the AR and bidirectional branches compare at equal compute remains unresolved by the retrieved evidence [13][14].

**Unification without a settled objective.** Discrete early fusion [15][16][50], hybrid NTP-plus-diffusion/flow [17][18], decoupled encoders [19][20] and discrete flow matching [51] all claim to be the right unification. The disagreements are concrete: quantisation-bounded reconstruction fidelity [15] versus the claimed scaling advantage of continuous patches [17]; shared encoders versus decoupled ones [19][20]; capacity separation via experts [18]. None of the retrieved models is a genuine four-modality generator: video and audio are mostly inputs or extra token streams [15][18][50], and true joint audio-video generation is a separate, newer line [23][21][22].

**Evaluation is the bottleneck.** Three findings are hard to reconcile with current practice. First, realism and understanding are decoupled: a benchmark built on real-world footage concludes that visual realism does not imply physical understanding [28]. Second, scaling does not close the gap: perfect in-distribution but failing out-of-distribution generalisation, with case-based imitation rather than rule abstraction [29], and explicit statements that scaling and prompt engineering are insufficient [27]. Third, the judges themselves are fragile: documented cross-modal hallucination and score ambiguity in MLLM judges [30], reward models acknowledged as black boxes with biases [60], and human protocols that are inconsistent, unstandardised and expensive [31], against benchmarks with limited coverage and no single suite spanning all axes [34][26]. Provenance tooling is developing in parallel rather than ahead: open watermarking frameworks target robustness to compression and temporal attacks, the manipulations that matter most for video [61].

**Open versus closed.** Open releases are now quantitatively competitive on visual quality [9][10][11][45], but the gap persists in task coverage and efficiency [11], and closed systems are the preferred choice of human annotators in the controlled study available [31]. Whether open models can also be evaluated on equal terms depends on benchmarks whose outputs, prompts and annotations are released [24][26].

### Note on evidence quality

Most model entries in this survey rest on abstracts, technical reports, project pages and release notes rather than on full-text measurement tables, and the numbers quoted are those present in the retrieved abstracts, full texts or project pages. A follow-up verification pass re-fetched a sample of the cited pages and confirmed the LTX-Video latency figure, the VAR FID/IS/IS and inference-speed figures, the HunyuanVideo parameter count and human-evaluation comparison, the Sparse VideoGen2 speedup/PSNR pair, and the VBench-2.0 dimension count verbatim. The same pass could not re-confirm several statistic-level figures that live deep inside paper bodies beyond this session's fetch limits, so those figures were removed or de-attributed above; one benchmark's reported top score was contradicted by a secondary summary and was dropped entirely. A small number of records in the retrieval index carried publication dates after 2025, and a few evaluation and safety items could not be tied to a retrievable identifier, so neither set is used for any claim above.
